# DEDprotocol — Expert Sources Map

**Purpose.** A short map of the real expert literature around the framework: what it supports,
what it challenges, and how the paper may use it. This is **not** an endorsement of the RBDC
carrier protocol, and no source below validates the specific 144–456 Hz payload.

**Rule:** cite these as context and as inputs. Do not present them as evidence that the RBDC
payload works.

---

## 1. The field is real

**Levin M.**
*Bioelectrical approaches to cancer as a problem of the scaling of the cellular self.* Prog
Biophys Mol Biol. 2021;165:102–113. doi:10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2021.04.007.

- **Supports:** the perspective that cancer can be framed as a breakdown of bioelectrical
  coordination, with cells reverting toward unicellular objectives.
- **Challenges:** it is a perspective, not a primary measurement or a clinical result; it does
  not identify a specific carrier or validate RBDC.
- **Use in the paper:** cite in §3.1 as framing; explicitly say DED uses the weaker premise
  (Vmem is a correlate; bioelectric state can regulate patterning in model systems). Do not cite
  it as evidence for the 456/480.6 Hz carrier hypothesis.

**Chernet BT, Levin M.**
*Transmembrane voltage potential is an essential cellular parameter for the detection and
control of tumor development in a Xenopus model.* Dis Model Mech. 2013;6(3):595–607.
doi:10.1242/dmm.010835.

- **Supports:** in Xenopus, Vmem depolarization precedes oncogene-induced tumor-like structures;
  hyperpolarization can suppress or normalize them.
- **Challenges:** amphibian developmental model, not human tissue; the intervention is
  genetic/optogenetic, not the RBDC payload.
- **Use in the paper:** cite as model-system evidence for the weaker bioelectric premise, not as
  a clinical result.

**Mathews J, Erickson P, Kuchling F, Jawanda N, Pio-Lopez L, Pai VP, Levin M.**  
*Meeting Review: “National Cancer Institute Conference on Cancer Bioelectricity” September 12,
2024.* Bioelectricity. 2025;7(1):94–104. doi:10.1089/bioe.2024.0049. PMID 40342939.

- **Supports:** An NCI-sponsored meeting with 14 researchers; bioelectricity in cancer is a
  recognized research area. Michael Levin's framing of cancer as a loss of bioelectrical
  collective coordination; Vmem depolarization as an indicator/inducer; tools for reading and
  writing bioelectrical states.
- **Challenges:** The meeting itself notes that nonpharmacological bioelectric translation is
  hard, regulatory/reimbursement paths are not well defined, and more basic measurements are
  needed. It is **not** a clinical efficacy result.
- **Use in the paper:** cite as field context in the introduction. Say “the field is active and
  NCI-recognized,” not “NCI endorses this protocol.”

**Li K, Zhang Y, Qian Y, Qin H, Zhang H, Li C, Peng C, Zhang J, Feng S.**  
*Tumor Electrophysiology in Precision Tumor Therapy.* iMetaMed. 2025;1(1).
doi:10.1002/imm3.70002.

- **Supports:** Vm depolarization is a central electrophysiological hallmark of tumor cells;
  ion-channel fingerprinting; electric-field therapies; the link to proliferation, cancer stem
  cells, and metastasis.
- **Challenges:** Translational challenges and the need for long-term efficacy validation;
  electric-field therapy is not one thing — frequency, amplitude, and mechanism matter.
- **Use:** cite for the Vmem/depolarization premise and for the “electric field therapy is a
  real but complex modality” sentence.

## 2. The tissue-scale / nematic framing is real

**Gottheil P, Bhattacharyya S, Lettl K, Friedrich P, Roth K, Rivera-Moreno S, Merkel M,
Aktas B, Sauer I, Daneshgar A, Wieland J, Kubitschke H, Wegscheider A-S, Yeomans JM, Käs JA.**  
*Self organisation of invasive breast cancer driven by the interplay of active and passive
nematic dynamics.* arXiv:2412.01285 (2024). doi:10.48550/arXiv.2412.01285.

- **Supports:** Simulations and histology from **over 2,000 breast cancer patients** show cancer
  clusters moving as active nematic aggregates through aligned ECM; topological defects in ECM
  alignment near tumour boundaries; activity estimates correlate with progression and poorer
  prognosis. This is the strongest external support for the **tissue-scale nematic/topological**
  premise.
- **Challenges:** It describes self-organization and progression; it does **not** propose a
  corrective carrier, a base-38 encoding, or a therapeutic payload.
- **Use:** cite in the introduction/methodology as the physical-biology grounding for “malignant
  tissue can be modelled as an active nematic with topological defects.” Do not cite it as
  support for the RBDC output.

**Cheung KJ, Horne-Badovinac S.**  
*Collective cell migration modes in development, tissue repair and cancer.*
Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol. 2025;26(10):741–758. doi:10.1038/s41580-025-00858-9.

- **Supports:** Collective cancer invasion is a tissue-scale, emergent process; tumour clusters
  move collectively; the microenvironment matters.
- **Challenges:** Not a defect-specific or frequency-specific claim; it is context, not a
  mechanism for the carrier protocol.
- **Use:** one citation in the introduction for “cancer invasion is collective and tissue-scale.”

**Saw TB, et al.**  
*Topological defects in epithelia govern cell death and extrusion.* Nature. 2017;544:212–216.

- **Supports:** The foundational experimental result that topological defects in epithelial
  nematic order localize cell death/extrusion. Already cited in the manuscript.
- **Use:** keep as the primary defect citation. Do not extend it to “defects cause cancer” or
  “correcting defects cures cancer.”

## 3. The closest clinical modality is TTFields — and it is different

**Khagi S, Kotecha R, Gatson NTN, Jeyapalan S, Abdullah HI, Avgeropoulos NG, Batzianouli ET,
Giladi M, Lustgarten L, Goldlust SA.**  
*Recent advances in Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields) therapy for glioblastoma.*
The Oncologist. 2025;30(2):oyae227. doi:10.1093/oncolo/oyae227.

- **Supports:** TTFields is an FDA-approved, clinically validated electric-field modality at
  **100–300 kHz**, with a multimodal mechanism (mitotic disruption, DNA damage response,
  motility, immune effects). It proves that electric fields can have biological and clinical
  effects.
- **Challenges:** TTFields operates **three orders of magnitude above** the RBDC 144–456 Hz
  band, and its mechanism is mitotic/dielectrophoretic, not “topological reversion.” The review
  does **not** support the RBDC carrier protocol. It is the wrong modality to cite as proof.
- **Use:** cite in the limitations/discussion to make the orthogonal-modality argument explicit:
  “TTFields is the established clinical comparator; RBDC is a different, untested regime.”

## 4. What the search did not find

- No peer-reviewed clinical evidence for the specific **144–456 Hz** RBDC carrier protocol.
- No peer-reviewed evidence that **base-38** has biological significance.
- No peer-reviewed evidence that the **PE-IM-ER payload** produces topological reversion in
  tissue.
- No expert source that endorses the specific mapping from the profile fields to the carrier set.

That absence is the paper's honest gap. It does not make the computational work wrong; it makes
the biological effect a hypothesis.

## 5. What to add to the paper

1. **Introduction:** cite Mathews et al. 2025 and Li et al. 2025 for the field and the Vmem
   premise; cite Gottheil et al. 2024 for the active-nematic/topological framing; cite Cheung &
   Horne-Badovinac 2025 for collective invasion.
2. **Limitations:** cite Khagi et al. 2025 for TTFields as the established clinical modality and
   state the frequency/mechanism difference explicitly.
3. **Claims ledger:** add rows for these sources as “cited input/context,” not as results.
4. **Do not:** cite the NCI meeting as an endorsement; cite TTFields as proof of RBDC; cite the
   active-nematic paper as proof of the carrier; use unpublished exploratory material as
   evidence.
